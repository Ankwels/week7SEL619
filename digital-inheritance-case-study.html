DIGITAL INHERITANCE

Digital inheritance on blockchain

How could a person’s online assets be protected and passed on after death?

In 2025, researchers examined how blockchain could help people plan what happens to their personal digital assets after death.

The examples include crypto assets, NFTs and personal email accounts (Hernando-Corrochano et al., 2025).

What the 2025 study examines

The authors focus on personal digital assets people may want to pass on after death.

Personal digital assets

Examples include cryptocurrency, NFTs and personal email accounts.

Work accounts

The authors exclude work email, describing it as an organisation’s account rather than the employee’s personal asset.

Key point: digital inheritance is broader than transferring cryptocurrency. It can also involve records, files and accounts held by online services.

The article describes its scope as citizens’ personal and private digital assets (Hernando-Corrochano et al., 2025).

What blockchain could contribute

The authors propose using a shared record to store inheritance instructions and the conditions for carrying them out.

1 · PlanChoose what should happen to each asset
2 · RecordSave the instructions and conditions
3 · CheckConfirm the required information
4 · ActPass on the asset or follow the plan

Potential advantages in the article

A durable record, clear conditions, easier auditing, and more control for the person planning ahead.

What the article argues

Blockchain may improve the speed and security of managing digital inheritance.

These are the paper’s proposed benefits; the abstract does not give measured results for speed or cost.

Crypto and email need different steps

Crypto or an NFT

  • Recorded on a blockchain
  • Access depends on digital keys
  • A blockchain system may be able to transfer it under set conditions

Personal email or files

  • Stored with an online service
  • Access is controlled by that service
  • The provider may need to verify a request and grant access
Practical implication: a blockchain can keep instructions, but it cannot by itself transfer an account that is controlled by a separate company.

The study includes personal email as a digital-legacy example; the distinction above follows from where each asset or account is controlled (Hernando-Corrochano et al., 2025).

The system must connect to the outside world

A blockchain cannot independently check a death record, bank balance or property register.

1 · Official recordDeath is recorded
2 · Trusted checkA service confirms the information
3 · InstructionsBlockchain rules are checked
4 · Existing systemsBank or property records are updated
TheStreet interviewee Nick Scherling said the idea would need to connect to traditional financial systems—or replace them—and that this connection was not yet in place (Nelson, 2023).

This interview is industry commentary, not a test of the research design. It highlights the work needed to connect digital instructions with banks and property records.

Main takeaway and references

Blockchain may help keep clear instructions for digital inheritance. Passing assets on still depends on keys, trusted checks, service providers and the relevant legal process.
